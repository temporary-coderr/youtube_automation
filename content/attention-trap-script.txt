WISE-LAB — THE ATTENTION TRAP
Target runtime: 120 seconds
Format: 16:9 landscape
Narration pace: calm, clear, approximately 125–135 words per minute

[SCENE 1 — CITY WALK — 0:00–0:20]
Every day, your attention is pulled in a hundred directions. A notification lights up. Someone passes. A thought appears. And without deciding, you follow it. The problem isn't that your mind wanders. The problem is that you rarely notice the moment it happens.

[SCENE 2 — PHONE DISTRACTION — 0:20–0:40]
Imagine walking home with one simple plan: get home, make dinner, and finish one important task. Then your phone vibrates. You reach for it almost automatically. One message becomes three. A quick check quietly turns into ten lost minutes.

[SCENE 3 — QUIET ROOM — 0:40–1:00]
This is how distraction works. It doesn't need to defeat your goals. It only needs to interrupt them often enough. Each interruption leaves a little mental residue. Part of your attention stays with the message, even after the screen goes dark.

[SCENE 4 — DESK / FOCUS — 1:00–1:20]
Try a small experiment. Put the phone beyond arm's reach. Choose one task that matters. Make the first step so clear that you can begin in less than a minute. You are not relying on stronger willpower. You are changing the environment around your attention.

[SCENE 5 — PARK / RESET — 1:20–1:40]
When the urge to check appears, pause. Notice it. Take one slow breath, and return to the next physical action. You don't have to fight every thought. You only have to decide which thought deserves your next move.

[SCENE 6 — SUNSET / WALK FORWARD — 1:40–2:00]
Focus is not never getting distracted. It is getting better at coming back. Every return is a repetition. Make the useful choice easier, make the distraction harder to reach, and protect one small block of time today. That's how attention becomes a skill. This is WISE-LAB.
